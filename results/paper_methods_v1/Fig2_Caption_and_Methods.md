# Fig. 2. Actual training and design workflow

**Caption.** Workflow for the original 100K, 15-GHz transformer model, seed 20260713. (a) Existing paired geometry and EM-response data retain their original geometry-grouped train, validation and test assignments. Geometry and response scalers are fitted to the training partition only. (b) The forward network maps ten normalized geometry coordinates to four responses. Its relative-MSE objective uses stored EM labels, including the exact extracted Qmin. Validation selects the forward checkpoint. (c) The inverse network maps normalized electrical targets to geometry through the original constrained decoder, `hard_feasible_topology_v1`. The selected forward network remains frozen, but its derivative with respect to geometry is retained. The inverse objective combines scheduled response consistency, a 0.01 geometry-anchor term and a 0.02 analytical topology term. Its Q response term penalizes shortfall to Qreq+2 during training; the additional 2 is not added to inference targets. (d) A target produces one decoded geometry. A separate frozen-forward diagnostic neither selects nor modifies it. Candidate-bound layout, DRC and EM verification are separate physical stages. Failures remain reportable. Neither EM solving nor candidate search is part of a gradient update or the one-shot inverse call.

## Definitions and exact implementation

The response vector is `[Lp, Ls, Qmin, |k|]`, with inductances in nH, Qmin=min(Qp,Qs), and dimensionless coupling magnitude. The ten geometry coordinates and their source order are preserved in the existing structure package; geometric lengths are in µm. `Sg(g)=(g−μg)/σg` and `Sr(r)=(r−μr)/σr`; the means/scales and decoder bounds remain attached to each model's own training data and weight identity.

Original F:10→256→256→128→4,102020 parameters. Original I:4→512→512→256→10,399114 parameters. Hidden layers use GELU. F has a linear normalized-response output; I produces logits for the coupled analytical decoder. The decoder enforces its recorded geometry envelope and selected topology relations; it is not full foundry DRC and does not guarantee physical target achievement.

The original response loss uses mean-normalized feature weights[0.5,0.5,1,2] and physical denominator floors[0.5nH,0.5nH,5,0.05]. For F, all four labels are exact. For I, Lp/Ls/|k| use signed prediction-minus-target residuals, while Q uses `min(Qpred−Qreq−2,0)`. The response coefficient λ(t) follows the saved warmup/ramp/adaptive schedule; it is not constant. The geometry anchor acts in normalized geometry coordinates. The response derivative crosses the frozen F input Jacobian, then the full coupled decoder Jacobian, before Adam updates I. No F optimizer step occurs during I training. The NumPy implementation explicitly computes these derivatives; the diagram does not imply a PyTorch `no_grad` graph.

The original split contains78891 gradient-training,9740 validation and11369 reserved-test rows. The group split seed is20260711. The model seed is20260713; the original inverse sampling RNG is seed20260714, not a separate model identity. Checkpoint selection uses validation, not common-test outcomes. The source-name phrase “response_only” does not remove the nonzero anchor and topology terms.

- **Dataset-total comparison:** D10k uses8000/1000/1000; D50k uses40000/5000/5000; D100k retains78891/9740/11369. Small partitions are nested within their original parent partitions. All retain the original asymmetric F/I architecture above. D10k and D50k start from scratch; D100k reuses the exactly matched original selected checkpoints and embedded normalizer. The common forward test is the original11369 test rows. This is not100000 gradient-training rows.
- **Width-only comparison:** Small, Medium and Large each train their own F then I on the same original100K partition. Their three hidden layers have widths128,256 and512, respectively, in both F and I. These are full paired F/I systems, not inverses trained through one shared newly trained F. This family is separate from the asymmetric dataset-total comparison and from the earlier6329 development experiments.

This figure gives method definitions, not experiment completion or performance claims. Updated numerical results belong to their terminal-bound tables. Own-F diagnostics and common-F scoring are proxy evidence; they are not fresh EM. Physical evaluation reports must separately bind the candidate, original target, actual layout and EM response.

## Graph tables and source scope

`workflow_nodes.csv` and `workflow_edges.csv` retain the more detailed existing source graph, including source selection, loss branches, validation selection and deployment. The displayed figure groups adjacent normalization/model and output/loss operations for readability. Historical data formation includes a separate bootstrap LHS stage. The main upstream pool contains 1,200,000 proposals from three generation modes: 600,000 sparse-neighborhood perturbations, 240,000 rare-marginal-neighborhood perturbations and 360,000 global-LHS proposals. After geometry feasibility screening, five acquisition quotas select the 120,000-candidate execution queue; these quotas are selection criteria, not five proposal-generation algorithms. Capacity-aware water-fill selection then chooses the original 100,000 members from the 120,810-entry accepted parent pool. The selected 100K contains 3,590 bootstrap and 96,410 main-production members; its exact selected per-generation-mode contributions remain unknown. It is not a pure LHS dataset. No source distribution or sampling algorithm is inferred from a histogram.
